# 探索と経路計画 (Search & planning)

隣接セル読み取り (#89)、unknown の扱い、斜行せず横移動する方針 (#76)、経路チェック、壁を消さない規則。

> CLAUDE.md から移した知見ページ。索引は [README.md](README.md)。
> 新しい知見はこのページ (または該当ページ) に追記し、CLAUDE.md には足さない。

- **Search and speed runs treat unknown cells in *opposite* ways.** `flood_fill` (#18) is deliberately optimistic — an unseen wall is `False` in `Maze`, so unexplored cells read as open and the mouse goes and looks. `shortest_path` (#19) must be pessimistic: pass `known=explorer.visited` so the route only crosses cells whose walls were actually observed. Racing through an unobserved cell is how you hit a wall you never saw. `Maze` has no tri-state for walls; **the `known` set is what carries "unknown"**, so any new consumer has to thread it through.
- **The machine does not turn — it strafes** (#76, the default since M6). It is holonomic and the downward camera sees all four edges at once, so heading is held at maze north for the whole run and a "turn" is just a change of translation axis. This **halves everything**: measured on the same 5×5, search 73.1 → 37.7 s and the speed run 51.6 → 24.6 s (1.94× / 2.10×), and the map came out identical. Consequences worth knowing:
  - `body_walls_to_maze()` degenerates to a constant mapping (FRONT→N, BACK→S, LEFT→W, RIGHT→E), so `Explorer` tracks `travel` (last direction moved) instead of `facing`, and `flood_fill`'s "fewest quarter-turns" tie-break now means "don't change the travel axis".
  - **In-place turns are kept, not deleted** (`--turn-in-place` on both run scripts, `LEGACY_COST`). Beyond being an escape route, `gyro_scale_z` is calibrated with `cell_move_demo --seq Q,Q,Q,Q --camera-yaw`; delete the turn primitive and you lose the only way to calibrate the gyro. Never mix the two modes within a run — derive both from one flag.
  - The 13 in-place turns per run were ~1170° of gyro integration and 5–25 mm of rotation slip. Dropping them should make `conflicts` and the measured `cell_offset` residuals *better*, not just faster; if they get worse, something else is wrong.
  - **Diagonals remain impossible** and this does not change that: a 45° corridor is 110.3 mm wide and the machine's circumscribed circle is 125.2 mm.
- **Reading the neighbours buys stops, not distance — and the stop that a turn costs is not one of them** (#89, on by default, `--no-neighbors` to go back). At 960×720 the E/W neighbours' far walls (270 mm out) fall just inside the 281 mm half-frame, so one frame determines those cells completely and the mouse can cross them without stopping. Measured over the 31 contest mazes (medians, paired): **stops 90 → 73**, cells 90 → 93 in the earlier run but **−4 in the paired comparison** (the route does not get worse on average even though passed-through cells reveal nothing), search time −8 % at zero per-stop overhead and **−11 % at 0.5 s/stop**. 26 of the 31 improved, 4 got worse, 1 unchanged. Three things are worth knowing before re-deriving any of this:
  - **The issue's own estimate of 85 → 55 counted *observations*, not stops.** With the current primitives a direction change still costs a full stop (`CellMotion` decelerates to zero), and only same-direction cells merge into one motion. Counting "cells entered whose walls were already known" reproduces the issue's number exactly (85 → 52); the 21-stop gap between that and 73 is what **#80 (turning without stopping)** would unlock, not something a better wall reading can reach.
  - **`known` is now a bigger set than `visited`, and the speed run routes over it.** A cell whose four walls were seen from the outside is safe to race through; `RunManager._route` and `search_run`'s report pass `explorer.known` (formerly `visited`). On 16×16 that is 130 known cells against 87, and the number of contest mazes that fit a speed run inside 7 minutes went **17/31 → 23/31**. The speed-run time itself is a wash (median +0.00 s; 13 mazes faster, 8 slower).
  - **The far bands read *better* than the machine's own edges, and the safety guard fires exactly where predicted** (measured on the 5×5, five frames per pose). With walls: neighbour front/back **0.46–0.48**, far side **0.81–0.82** (the strongest ROI on the machine — the far ROI is deliberately thin, 16 mm, so a 12 mm band fills most of it); without: **0.00** on all three. Frame-to-frame spread is 0.00. The neighbour's back ROI needs none of the shortening the machine's own BACK ROI needs, because the ribbon cable is not out at ±180 mm: it reads 0.47. Sliding the machine right off centre took the left far band **0.82 → 0.36 (still detected) → 0.00**, at a *measured* lateral offset of +4.7 / −8.2 / −15.2 mm — so a fully clipped band really does read zero, and at −15.2 mm the guard refused the "no wall" verdict and dropped the cell to 3/4 instead. The guard trips at −6.7 mm while detection survives to about −15 mm, i.e. it is ~13 px conservative; that margin is worth keeping, because a badly-seated wall reads weak on its own (#88 saw 0.24).
  - **The 5×5 confirmed it end to end, and every run produced the *identical* map** (same ASCII, same 20-cell / 12-leg shortest path at cost 33.5, same cell order). 24 moves either way; **stops 24 → 20**, wall-clock **38 → 35 s**, which is the simulation's prediction (−19 % stops, −8 %) landing on hardware. Two neighbour-reading runs an hour apart matched each other on *every* discrete output and on the wall-clock second. The four two-cell moves took **1.87 s** against 2 × 1.12 s plus a stop, i.e. ~0.85 s saved each. The path check printed "1 セル目までしか見えない" for the N/S pairs and stayed quiet for the E/W ones, so the far-band check really is running wherever it can.
  - **A two-cell move is no worse for heading than a one-cell move — the first run said otherwise and was wrong.** One 0.99° residual on a two-cell move looked like "longer motion, more rotation", but over two runs the eight two-cell moves average **0.32°** (max 0.99°) while the largest residual of either run, **1.09°**, was on a *one-cell* move. Nothing here argues against raising `--pass-cells`; the limit on that is the correction interval, not the motion. This is the "repeat a surprising measurement before theorising about it" rule catching a claim that had already been written down.
  - **The frame now reaches a cell beyond the maze, so anything red stored next to it is *in the picture*.** At (4,0) the `right:right` slot — one cell past the east wall — read **0.97**, and both runs (with and without the neighbour ROIs) rejected the axis-yaw correction at exactly (4,0) and (4,1) with +5.4…+7.4°. The cause was mundane: spare walls, spare posts and a **red ruler bag** were piled east of the maze. Nothing reached the map (`Explorer.observe` drops out-of-bounds neighbours and the 5° guard rejected every bad yaw), but the lesson generalises past #89 — at 960×720 the frame covers **3.15 × 2.33 cells**, so the "harmless pile beside the maze" is now inside it, and it lands in the axis-yaw line fit, which has no notion of being out of bounds. **Clear a one-cell margin around the maze before a run**, and read an unexplained yaw rejection at a boundary cell as "look at what is on the floor over there" first. Tidying up settled it: the same run then read `right:right` **0.00** at (4,0) and accepted the yaw at both cells (**+0.37° and +1.32°**, against +5.44° and +7.28° rejected before).
  - **A neighbour reading has three outcomes, not two** (`NEIGHBOR_CLEAR_MAX_FRACTION`, `neighbor_walls`). Writing an unconfirmed edge as "no wall" would make that cell `known` and therefore **passable at speed** — the exact failure this feature must not have. So a fraction between the clear limit (0.04) and the wall threshold (0.08) writes nothing, and the cell simply stays a normal stop. The dangerous case is specific: the far band sits 14.5 px from the frame edge, and while a **half-clipped** band still reads ≈0.2–0.3 (detected fine), a **fully clipped** one reads 0.00 with a flat search profile, so it raises no saturation flag and looks exactly like an open edge. That needs ~15 mm of lateral offset, which is inside what this machine can drift, so the far edge additionally requires the machine's measured lateral offset (from its own side bands, `lateral_shift_px`) to put the far band's centre at least 3 px inside the frame — and refuses to say "no wall" when that offset cannot be measured at all.
- **Before moving, re-check that the path is clear** (`search_run --no-front-check` to disable). The planner only ever picks a direction the *map* says is open, so a wall appearing in the live frame means the **pose is wrong**, not the map. Aborting there is what keeps a pose error from becoming a series of collisions. Which body edge to look at now depends on the direction of travel, not the facing — `wall_detect.body_edge_for(direction, facing)`. **The check has its own, higher threshold** (`PATH_BLOCK_MIN_FRACTION = 0.20`) because it asks a different question from wall detection: detection has no prior and a miss is a collision (so 0.08), while the path check has a *strong prior that the path is open* and a false positive throws away a good run — one did, on a RIGHT reading of 0.09. Measured separation is 0.121 (no wall) vs 0.302 (wall), and the genuinely dangerous case (pose drifted, wall unseen) reads 0.00, so raising it costs almost no detection power. Same shape as `OFFSET_MIN_FRACTION`; keep the thresholds separate when tuning.
- **Never let a wall *observation* clear a wall the map already knows** (`strategy/explorer.py`). Two safety valves, both earned on hardware: (1) **outer walls are read-only** — the first real `search_run --dry-run` had the camera call the boundary behind the robot "no wall" and erase it from the map; (2) `sticky_walls` (default) keeps an already-seen wall when a later look says "absent", counting it in `Explorer.conflicts` instead. A false negative drives the machine into a wall; a false positive only costs a detour, so the asymmetry is deliberate. A rising `conflicts` means pose error or mis-detection, not a maze that changed.
