# 機構・故障・停止 (Mechanics, faults & stopping)

L6470 の状態ポーリング、ボタンランチャー、非常停止 (シグナル)、励磁の跳ね、緩んだ締結、ローラーの擦れ、異音 4 原因の対照表。

> CLAUDE.md から移した知見ページ。索引は [README.md](README.md)。
> 新しい知見はこのページ (または該当ページ) に追記し、CLAUDE.md には足さない。

- **Every driving script polls `GetStatus` after each primitive** (`l6470.fault_flags`, `tuning.describe_faults`). GetStatus clears the flags on read, so a poll per primitive attributes `STEP_LOSS`/`OCD`/`TH_WRN` to the move that caused it. Note L6470 stall detection is BEMF-based and insensitive at low speed: **a flag means trouble, but no flag does not mean no slip**.
- **The button launcher adds no stop path of its own** (#79, `app/launcher.py`, `hal/gpio_io.py`, `scripts/launcher.py`). A press while running sends the child `SIGINT`, i.e. exactly Ctrl-C through `emergency_stop`; if the child is still alive 2 s later it gets `SIGTERM` (same handler), and after that **only an alarm** — never `SIGKILL`, which would leave the L6470s holding their last Run command. What stops the motors then is the **VS cut-off toggle**, which is why that toggle is hardware and not software. The run command is `config/run.yaml` (gitignored), written only by `speed_run --save-run-config` **on a completed session**, and stored as the verbatim argument list rather than as per-setting fields, so what runs on the day is exactly what was validated at the trial; the launcher re-parses it with the script's own `build_parser` at boot and logs the whole command, EV included. Long-press events only count for a press that *began* while idle — otherwise holding the stop press through the end of the run would read as "held 1 s and released" and restart it. `speed_run` now exits **2** on an aborted session (the launcher's beep tells finished from aborted by the exit code). The boot-time camera check the issue sketched was dropped: the AWB lock belongs to the start cell (#125), which is where `speed_run` opens the camera anyway.
- **Stopping the machine is a signal problem, not a `finally` problem** (`motion/emergency_stop.py`). `with L6470Chain(...)` releases the coils on a clean exit, but `timeout`'s **SIGTERM** and some **SIGINT** paths skip it, and the L6470 then holds its last Run command — the machine spun until power was cut. Every driving script wraps the chain in `emergency_stop(chain)`, which releases (`soft_stop_all` → `hard_hiz_all`, in that order) *inside the signal handler* and then re-raises for the default action. **SIGKILL cannot be caught**: `python -m scripts.motor_stop` is the recovery, or cut VS. And **ESC does nothing** — these scripts read no keyboard; say Ctrl-C when telling someone how to stop.
- **A dead SPI link must end the run, not trigger a recovery** (#148, `hal.l6470.SpiLost`, `motion.tuning.raise_if_spi_lost`, `app.exit_codes`). On 2026-10-16 (8×8, after the roof work) all three drivers read `NO_SPI` after a chained motion; the 2.5° guard saw 6.77° of uncommanded rotation and the #113 recovery re-posed the machine and sent the next leg — which never arrived, so the drivers kept their last Run and the machine turned **another 16.42°** before the once-per-session cap stopped it. `spi_watch` then found 3 dropouts in 30 s while the wires were wiggled. Recovery assumes commands get through; with no link that assumption is false and every retry is uncontrolled driving. Now any device reading 0x0000/0xFFFF after a primitive (`speed_run` / `search_run` / `cell_move_demo`) sends HardHiZ for up to 1 s (the link drops in and out, so a release can land when it comes back), then exits with **`EXIT_SPI_LOST` = 3**, and the launcher sounds **`BEEP_ALARM`** instead of the "aborted" beep — the operator's stop is the VS toggle, because `motor_stop` goes over the same SPI. The old wording "トルク不足の疑い" pointed the wrong way for this flag. Start-up SPI failures also no longer exit 0 (the launcher used to play "done").
- **Energize before you measure the starting pose** (`VelocityDriver.energize`). The L6470 sits in HiZ after reset and only energises on the first motion command, at which point each rotor snaps to its nearest detent and the chassis jumps. Measured at **+2.06°** — though most of that turned out to be a loose hub screw (below); with the joints tight it is `−0.03 ± 0.26°`, i.e. the detent snap alone is under the measurement noise. Measure first and that jump is misfiled as "rotation during the move", which is exactly what made the gyro and the camera disagree by up to 1.6° on pure straights. Every driving script now issues a zero-velocity command and settles before the first camera reading.
- **A loose fastener will masquerade as an irreducible mechanical limit — check the joints before you design around one** (#72). A `+2.06°` chassis jump on energize, a 0.9–1.45° residual at the end of every turn, and ±1.2° of scatter in the crab estimate all fitted the arithmetic for the omni wheel's roller clearance (rollers have 0.5–1.0 mm of slop along the wheel's rotation direction; the contact point is `center_to_wheel_m` = 45.07 mm from the centre, so the chassis can rotate **θ = play / 45.07 mm = 0.64–1.27°** with the wheels held still). The fit was good and the conclusion — "inherent to the wheel, you cannot control your way through it" — was wrong. **The front wheel's hex hub screw was loose.** After tightening it the energize jump went to `−0.03 ± 0.26°` (5-frame means; consistent with zero), while the turn residual and overshoot did *not* change — proving those were never play (see #73). This wheel takes a separately-sold hub through a hex socket, so there are three joints in series before the rollers (shaft↔hub, hub↔wheel, roller↔plate); measure them separately by holding a different part each time. **W0's spoke angle is ~0°, i.e. it drives purely sideways** (`body_to_wheels(0,1,0)` ≈ 1.0 for W0), so slop there lands directly on the lateral axis — which is why the crab calibration would not converge until it was fixed (scatter 1.2° → 0.05°). **But the rollers were not innocent** — see the next bullet (#103): they *bind* as well as wobble, and the play arithmetic above only covers the wobble.
- **The omni wheel's rollers have rubber longer than their cores, and it fouls the wheel plate — direction-dependently** (#103). The rollers are over-moulded (plastic core, rubber surface) and in places the rubber stood proud of the core; pushed axially, that lip touches the wheel plate and resists rotation. A roller is pushed axially exactly when its wheel is **skewed to the direction of travel**, and the *side* it is pushed toward follows the sign of the cross-track component — so an overhang on one side of one roller produces friction that depends on which way the machine is going. That is the shape #103 could not explain: all four rounded corners demand the same wheel speeds to within 1 % (peak 0.230–0.233 m/s), yet only **S→E** ever aborted, at a consistent **+1.3° of uncommanded rotation against the 2.5° guard**. **The requirement was symmetric; the resistance was not.** Four things worth keeping:
  - **It was found by ear, and the reasoning that got there is reusable.** Forward and backward drive the *same* two wheels at the *same* speeds (W1 355 / W2 344 step/s, sign flipped — `body_to_wheels` confirms it), so a noise present in only one direction **cannot** be torque, KVAL, current or supply, and must be mechanical. `cell_move_demo --seq F4` against `--seq B4` is the whole experiment; it took an hour of listening and no instrument.
  - **The bench test is to push a roller to one side by hand and turn it** — that reproduces a skewed wheel on the desk. Several wheels were then plainly stiff. Trimming the proud rubber cut the noise sharply and shrank the drift when it did occur.
  - **This partly reverses #72.** #72 fitted the same class of symptom to roller *clearance*, found a loose hub screw instead, and wrote the rollers off. The rollers were involved after all — by **binding, not slop**. A roller has two independent failure modes and `play / 45.07 mm` only covers one; **"we already ruled the rollers out" was ruling out one mechanism, not the part.**
  - **The noise is fixed; the rotation is a different fault that was riding along with it** (#107). Two trims later the rub is **audibly gone entirely**, and the direction-dependent rotation is exactly where it started: measured as `(B,D − F,A)/2` over four points — **0.83° → 0.62° → 0.86° → 0.92°** — it survived a coupling re-seat, the 0.24 → 0.30 m/s change, two rubber trims, and **a full removal and reshuffle of every roller** (orientations and positions deliberately not reproduced). **Shuffle the rollers and it does not move, so the rollers are not making it.** (Caveat: widespread residual overhang would also survive a reshuffle in aggregate — but paired with the noise being gone, "two faults, not one" is the natural reading.) **And it matters less than the chase implied**: 0.9° is 180 mm × sin 0.9° = **2.8 mm** of cross-track on the following leg, it is well under the 2.5° abort guard, and `apply_axis_heading` clears it at every stop — so it is *not* a candidate for "grazes a wall once every few sessions". **#103's pair was a good metric for the question #103 asked** (does the run abort?) **and the wrong one for #107's** (does it graze?); carrying it over was the mistake. Judge the trimming by the bench test — push a roller sideways and turn it — and judge the outcome by a full 8×8 session. If 0.9° is ever chased on its own, the discriminating run is **single legs with no `--chain`** in all four directions: antisymmetric across opposite directions points at wheel geometry (`wheel_diameters_m` has W0 at +1.5 % and was only ever calibrated from forward runs), present only when chained points at the blend.
- **The camera roof on four bare M3 rods was a mechanism, not a structure — it slipped into a twist and stayed there** (#143). The roof (water-washable resin, SLA) sits ~25 cm up on four M3 threaded rods, clamped between nuts. The trial day found its screws loose and the camera turned ~2.8° ([hardware-sessions](hardware-sessions.md)); afterwards, **just handling the machine into its box twisted the roof visibly, and it did not spring back**. Three things to keep:
  - **Loosening came in both kinds.** Turning loose under transverse vibration (700 km in a car boot) *and* clamp load lost to the resin creeping, which no lock nut prevents — the clamp runs through the resin, and a summer boot reaches the ~50 °C at which such resins soften. Re-tighten a day after fitting, then mark the nuts.
  - **The joints, not the rods, let go.** A nut on resin resists tilting only with clamp force × ~2.5 mm (half the nut's width), against a hand's force acting on a 25 cm lever. Estimated (M3 stainless, root Ø 2.4 mm, fixed–fixed): a rod stays elastic to ~10 mm of side-shift, so a twist that stays put means the ends tilted. With both ends free to tilt, four parallel rods form a parallelogram linkage with no torsional stiffness at all. Rod tilt reaches the camera multiplied by L/r (≈5 for a 25 cm rod 5 cm off-centre).
  - **Only a triangle stops it, so the fix is a brace, not a jig.** A locating jig was drawn twice and both versions failed: a pin-ended column trapped itself (it could not come out without lifting the roof), and a slotted one just tilted with the rods — the same weakness as the rods. Fitted: **X-braces of 0.45 mm stainless wire across the top ~5 cm only** (out of the camera's view — confirmed, the wires do not appear in `camera_fov` frames), anchored with M3 ring terminals clamped at the nodes; a **double nut under the roof** (the height/tilt reference, also takes the brace load), a **nylon nut on top** (it only holds the roof down). **Twist test after fitting (2026-10-16, `wall_detect --measure 5 --ev -2`, cell inside the maze): axis yaw −0.020° before, +0.028° after twisting the roof both ways by hand and letting go** — a 0.05° change, inside the 0.10–0.14° frame-to-frame spread, and the wall offsets did not move by a single 1.2 mm step; before the brace the same handling left a visible twist. Estimated stiffness gain ~8× over the slipping joints, *if* the rods sit without play in the two lower plates — that play now sets the remaining twist. **No thread-locker near the resin**: it can craze acrylate resins, cannot be released by heat there, and cures slowly on stainless.
- **An audible rub has had four different causes, and on the one chased hardest the first two guesses were both wrong** (#87 / `fed30ba` / #80 / #103). The symptom is identical every time: a scraping noise during a run, sometimes with distance missing, and **nothing whatsoever in the log** — no L6470 fault (BEMF stall detection is insensitive at this speed) and an odometry residual of 0.4–0.5 mm, because odometry integrates *commanded* speeds. The noise is the only live signal and it does not say what is rubbing, so the causes are collected here rather than each under its own subsystem:

  | cause | what rubs | the tell |
  |---|---|---|
  | **Floor board on the chassis underside** (#87, fixed in `fed30ba`) | the competition floor is a *board* and it flexes; at 0.30 m/s the machine shakes enough for it to touch | cost **27 mm along-track** on one 4-cell move. Pack was **full at 12.17 V** and the operator saw no wall contact — both guesses excluded. Fixed by lowering the battery (less shake) and adding spacers (less flex) |
  | **Roller rubber on the wheel plate** (#103, #107) | rubber standing proud of the roller core, once the roller is loaded axially | **direction-dependent**: audible going backward, silent going forward at the same wheel speeds. Bench test — push a roller sideways by hand and turn it |
  | **A run-down pack slipping** (#80) | the wheels, on the floor | only at low state of charge. Check the **LEDs** before a session; the 12 V rail is regulated so a voltmeter cannot tell you (`docs/venue-checklist.md`) |
  | **Actually grazing a wall** | the chassis, on a wall | along-track short *and* cross-track drift. On a socketed floor the wall does not topple, so watch the machine, not the maze |

  **The discrimination that works is not the table, it is one A/B**: `cell_move_demo --seq F4` against `--seq B4`. Forward and backward drive the *same* two wheels at the *same* speeds (W1 355 / W2 344 step/s, sign flipped), so anything appearing in only one direction is mechanical and **cannot** be supply, current, KVAL or torque. That is what found #103, by ear, in an hour. After that: full LEDs rules out the pack; an operator watching rules out a graze; happening on the black board but not the wood floor points at the board. **What you must not do is reason from "we have heard this noise before"** — the 27 mm event was called a graze, then a flat pack, before it turned out to be the floor board, and #103 was a fourth thing again. **Three of those four diagnoses were written down as fact at the time.**
- **`l6470.decode_status()`**: all-`0x0000`/all-`0xFFFF` ⇒ no SPI communication (wiring/power/CS), not a real fault; fault bits are active-low; UVLO on the first read after power-up is the normal power-up latch.
