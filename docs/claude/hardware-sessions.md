# 実機セッションの記録 (Hardware session log)

5×5・8×8 の通し走行、試走会 (2026-09-26) と本番 (2026-09-27) で何が起き、何を学んだか。

- **Rounding the corners takes a full 5×5 session from 25.0 s to 20.0 s per speed run, and the collision it was first blamed for was a flat battery** (#80, `--chain-legs 2`, the default since). Two five-run sessions back to back on the same day and the same charge: chained **20.1 / 20.0 / 20.0 / 19.9 s**, stopping at every leg **25.1 / 24.9 / 25.0 / 25.0 s** — **−20 %**, with the whole session 204 s against 244 s. Every per-move residual stayed under 1.4 mm, no run was aborted, and the maps came out identical. The earlier session that ended in a wall (first chunk **−15.2 mm and −26.09°**, correctly aborted by the 2.5° `--max-heading-residual` guard) ran the *same six chunks* cleanly four times first, so the geometry was never the problem. **The pack was the problem, and the way it fails is worth knowing**: a freshly charged pack reads 12.17 V and — measured before and after — **does not sag at all over a 204 s session** (12.17 → 12.18 V), so what matters is the state of charge you *start* with, not depletion during the run. On a run-down pack the machine slips audibly, and nothing in software sees it: no L6470 fault flag is raised (BEMF stall detection is insensitive at this speed) and the residuals stay at 0.5 mm because odometry integrates *commanded* speeds. **Measure the pack before a session**; a 5-run session is the load case that finds a weak one.
- **The 8×8 ran clean on the first attempt, and the estimates held to 0.5 %** (#23, `mazes/excerpt8.txt`). Search 22 moves / 20 stops / **33.3 s** against a predicted 33.1 s; four speed runs at **21.7 / 21.8 / 21.7 / 21.7 s** against a predicted 21.6 s; the whole 5-run session in 215 s. Every per-move residual stayed under 1.4 mm, nothing aborted, and the learned map was **exactly right over all 22 visited cells** — no missed wall, no invented one. The 12 walls it never saw are all in the quarter of the maze the search had no reason to enter.
  - **The tri-state neighbour reading earned its keep here** (#89). One far band read **0.05**, between the clear limit (0.04) and the wall threshold (0.08), so it was recorded as *undecided* and the cell stayed a normal stop. **There is a wall there.** Writing "no wall" would have made that cell `known` and therefore passable at speed.
  - **Four real walls read weakly, and it was the mask clipping them, not the seating** — 0.11, 0.18, 0.27, 0.30 where 0.40–0.55 is normal, all four detected. The first guess was "re-seat those walls"; the operator checked and they were seated fine, and they are **newly bought walls that look a slightly darker red**, which made "two wall lots" the obvious next theory. **It was neither.** #78 measured it: those walls photograph *paler*, not darker, and a pale band's hue drifts below `h2_lo`, so the mask was throwing away most of the band (see the `h2_lo` bullet under wall detection). Widening `h2_lo` 140 → 125 took the weakest wall from **1.1× the threshold to 5.5×** with no new false positives. The instinct that mattered was **not** tuning the threshold down to cover it — a lower threshold would have weakened the path check too, and would have treated a mask bug as a detection-sensitivity problem.
  - **`search_step_overhead_s` should be 0, not 0.5.** `straight_time_s` (0.83 s) already contains the 0.44 s stop, so adding a per-stop camera cost on top counts the stop twice and overestimates the search by 26 %. The 0.5 % agreement above is with the overhead at zero.
- **The second 8×8 found nothing wrong with the machine, and the board nobody planned found the real bug** (#85, `mazes/excerpt8_2015.txt`, wood floor, EV 0). Two sessions on the same day: a 5-run in **238 s** (search 39 moves / 32 stops / 58.8 s, speed runs 21.4 / 21.4 / 21.4 / 21.5 s) and a **25-run in 1102 s** (search 39 / 31 / 55.1 s, 24 speed runs at 20.8–20.9 s). Zero aborts, zero recoveries, no L6470 fault, and **all 28 speed runs took the identical route** — 23 cells / 13 legs, exactly what the simulation planned. The 1.8× longer search this board was chosen for (39 moves against 22) produced no new failure mode at all. What did produce one was a *hand-modified* board built afterwards to try the recovery path: its 6- and 7-cell corridors grazed walls, which is how the missing leg-length cap was found (see the `--max-leg-cells` bullet). **The planned experiment confirmed; the improvised one discovered.**
  - **`--save-frames` costs 0.09 s per stop, and it distorts exactly what it is measuring.** The 5-run session ran 2.6 % over the estimate and the 25-run session **−0.2 %** — same board, same route, same day. The *motion* times are identical (17.35 s against 17.40 s over the same seven motions); the whole difference is the per-stop overhead, **0.579 s against 0.486 s**. `capture()` writes a PNG on *every* capture, not only in the search, so that session wrote **185 frames while the CSV labels 33** — 150 of them are never used, and they are what made the search look 8 % slow. Read any timing fit as invalid if the flag was on.
  - **With the flag off the time model needs no change**: search **+1.4 %** (55.1 s against 54.4), speed run **−0.2 %** (20.8 s against 20.9), and the per-cell constant reproduces exactly — a 2-cell motion minus a 1-cell motion is **0.594 s** against the 0.60/0.61 in `RunManager`. The back-fitted fixed cost is 1.014 s (search) and 0.983 s (speed) against the 0.99 in the model.
  - **The camera has margin now, which is the number #78 said to read.** 306 labels, **zero misses and zero false alarms**, weakest wall **0.304 = 3.8× the threshold** (`back` at (2,0)), far bands 5.5–8.8×. #23 scored zero errors too while its weakest wall sat at **1.4×**; that was before `h2_lo` went to 125.
  - **The map was exactly right over everything it observed, and the 25-run session reproduced the simulation cell for cell** (39 moves / 31 stops / 34 visited / **51 known**). The 5-run session came out 32 stops / **49 known** — two neighbour edges landed in the undecided band, so those cells stayed a normal stop, which is the tri-state guard (#89) doing its job rather than a discrepancy. The only two walls the learned map lacks are on cells the search never entered and never confirmed, the same class as #23's twelve.
  - **The abort exposure is now 952 chained motions with zero guard trips**, i.e. **<0.32 % per motion** (rule of three), against 616 and 0.49 % before; 52 speed runs gives <5.8 % per run. The worst chained residual was **0.97°** (25-run) and 0.93° (5-run), and **both readings over 1.0° in the whole day were single-leg 2-cell motions** (1.03° and 1.05°) — the same pattern as #89's "a two-cell move is no worse than a one-cell move": whatever makes the tail, chaining is not it.
  - **Recovery ran in anger and the session finished all 5 runs** (#113). On the modified board a chained `N1→E1` left **+2.54°**, the 2.5° guard fired, `verify_cell` matched the believed cell, and the yaw fix (−2.93°, spread 0.46° over 15 segments / 1387 px) plus a −3.5 / +7.0 mm position fix put it back on the lattice; the run continued and the remaining runs were clean. **The cause was identified and mundane**: a short forward leg left the machine a few mm back, so the following eastward move clipped the post behind-right. That is a collision, not the unexplained #107 rotation — the tell is that the along-track residual on that leg was **6.3 mm** where every clean motion of the day sat under 1.7 mm.
- **The trial day (2026-09-26) ran a full 16×16 session, and the two things that nearly stopped it were both mechanical and both invisible to the checks that existed** (`venue/` work, `scripts/spi_watch.py`, `camera_yaw_offset_deg`). Final session: search 228.6 s (149 moves, one recovery), return, **speed run 63.3 s against an estimate of 63.0 s**, 357 s in all. The venue's start and goal tops were red. Three lessons:
  - **`NO_SPI` on all three drivers mid-run was a loose SPI connector, not VS.** The tell is in the next STATUS read: a VS dropout resets the L6470s and leaves **UVLO latched**; it was not, and M2 instead had **WRONG_CMD** set (a garbled frame). `spi_watch` (STATUS at 20 Hz while wiggling the wires) then reproduced it — 12 dropouts in 30 s, none after reseating. While SPI is down the drivers **keep their last Run command**, so the machine drives on uncontrolled until the post-move poll notices. The connector sits at header pins 19–26, next to the GND moved to pin 20 for the #79 button.
  - **A rotated camera passes the opposite-wall check and still steers the machine.** A loose roof screw let the camera turn ~2.8°. Opposite walls agreed to 0.0 mm (that check sees translation and scale, not rotation), but since every stop aligns the *camera* to the maze, the *body* ran 2.8° off: **every direction drifted to the right of travel, 9.6 mm per cell** (16.7 mm per 2 cells), corrections hit 24 mm, a back wall half-hidden under the machine read 0.074 and was driven into, and the earlier 4-cell strafe touched a wall. "Right of travel in all four directions, proportional to distance" is the signature of a heading-reference rotation (camera *or* spoke angle — the fix is the same). `robot.yaml`'s **`camera_yaw_offset_deg: 2.8`** is subtracted inside `apply_axis_heading`; after it the drift was −0.6 mm per cell and the per-stop offset median 2.1 mm. Check it with `cell_move_demo --seq F2 --align --camera-pose` after any transport.
  - **With the lateral scale fixed (#136), a leg leaving a corner falls 4–5 mm short in both directions** (3×3 black floor, `survey/check_w0`), and a leg entering one overshoots +3.6 mm — #80's original record, now separable from the W0 error. Both are absorbed by the next stop.
- **The competition (2026-09-27) was lost to the power-on procedure** (reproduced at home 2026-10-10). Stopping with the **Pi 5's power button (twice) leaves the X-UPS1's 5 V on**, and with `POWER_OFF_ON_HALT=0` the Pi waits halted but powered; pressing the **X-UPS1 switch does not restart it** — only the **Pi's own power button** does (15 s to the launcher's beep). The OS logs that would have shown this were gone: Raspberry Pi OS sets `Storage=volatile`, and the RTC has no backup battery, so offline boots resume from the last saved time. Now `POWER_OFF_ON_HALT=1` and a persistent journal (`docs/setup-pi5.md` §9); the procedure is in `docs/venue-checklist.md` §6. The surviving evidence was `/var/log/boot.log*` (rotated daily, several boots per file) and `logs/run_*.log`.
