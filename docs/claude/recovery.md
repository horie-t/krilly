# 姿勢の回復 (Recovery, #113)

`--max-heading-residual` で止まった後のその場回復: verify_cell、ガードの逆転、壁への接触 (lattice phase)、mod 90° の罠。

> CLAUDE.md から移した知見ページ。索引は [README.md](README.md)。
> 新しい知見はこのページ (または該当ページ) に追記し、CLAUDE.md には足さない。

- **An abort is recoverable in place, and the rule that looked like it forbade that applies to the *operator*, not the program** (#113, `localization/recovery.py`). When `--max-heading-residual` fires the machine is **already stopped** (the residual is judged after settling), so the camera is available. Rule 3-5's 「走行中止の申し出」 is a *human* request to the judge; a machine that re-establishes its own pose and drives on is not covered by it — at real contests a mouse that slips and recovers autonomously is fine, and the commentary treats recovering as a thing to credit. Beginners even build a straight edge into the chassis and push it against a wall to square up, which is legal (only hitting hard enough to break a wall risks disqualification). So **there is no return-to-start, no restart, and no run consumed** — fix the pose and continue. Three things make it work, and the third is the only hard one:
  - **Heading is exactly recoverable** (`median_axis_yaw` over ≥5 frames, σ≈0.1° at 960×720), because maze headings are always multiples of 90° so mod 90° is enough.
  - **Intra-cell position is recoverable to 2–3 mm** (`cell_offset`), but **only on axes that have a wall**.
  - **Which cell you are in is NOT recoverable from geometry** — the red bands repeat with the 180 mm pitch, so being one cell out looks identical. It is recovered from the **wall pattern** instead (`verify_cell`): compare the observed four edges against the learned map — **but only on the edges the map has actually observed** (`Explorer.observed`, threaded in as `known_edges`). `Maze` has no tri-state, so `has_wall` returning False means "no wall *or* never looked"; `flood_fill` may read that optimistically because it is going to go and look, and a *verifier* may not. Getting this wrong is not subtle — the first hardware run **stopped on the second move of the search** reporting "lost", because during a search almost every edge is unobserved and so every comparison fails. CLAUDE.md already said it ("the `known` set is what carries 'unknown', so any new consumer has to thread it through") and the new consumer did not. During the search the verdict is therefore usually *unverifiable* rather than *contradicted*, and the two must not be merged: unverifiable falls back to a different piece of evidence — the **along-track residual**, which excludes a whole-cell slip if it is under 30 mm (`CELL_SLIP_MAX_M`; the three recorded aborts were 10–14 mm, so a whole-cell slip has never actually been seen). It does not need to be globally unique, only to separate the believed cell from its **±1 neighbours along the travel axis**, which is the only error a distance slip can make. The believed cell wins when it matches (the prior is strong); a neighbour is taken only when the believed cell fails and exactly one neighbour matches. Zero matches means lost, two means indistinguishable — **both refuse**, and the honest failure case is two featureless neighbours (a wall perpendicular to the travel axis is not shared sideways, so E/W travel cannot use N/S walls to separate candidates).
  - **The guards invert in recovery mode, and that is the whole design.** `MAX_HEADING_CORRECTION_RAD` is 5° because *during a healthy run* a larger reading is a mis-measurement. In recovery we already know the pose is wrong, so the correction is allowed to 20° (`RECOVERY_MAX_HEADING_RAD`) and the gate moves to **measurement quality** instead — the frame-to-frame yaw spread must stay under **1.5°** (`RECOVERY_MAX_YAW_SPREAD_RAD`). Same asymmetry as the path check's 0.20 against detection's 0.08: **never reuse a number under a different prior.** Two corrections to that gate, both earned on the first hardware run: **0.5° was taken from #88's 0.11–0.26° and that figure was measured with the machine parked in one cell**, whereas in a run the stopping position varies and cells with fewer walls give shorter, fewer segments — 14 real recoveries spread **0.08–0.45°** and the 15th read 1.03° and ended the session. And **spread is a weak guard in the first place**: the dangerous failure is a *confident* wrong reading, which spread cannot see — the `axis_yaw` mis-read that applied a **−13.27° "correction"** and the red ruler bag's steady +5.4…+7.4° (#89) both had small spread. What actually protects the run is the 20° cap (bounding the damage), the **wall-pattern check** (independent evidence — the wall ROIs are position-based, so a yaw error does not corrupt them) and the **path check on the next move**. Tightening the spread gate does not stop the dangerous case; it only refuses healthy recoveries. The recovery log therefore prints segment count and total length, because without them a large-spread event cannot afterwards be told apart from "a cell with few walls".
  - **A jammed machine cannot read its own cell, and the two numbers that decide it are nearly equal** (#85, `perception/lattice.py`, `recovery.wall_contact`). The first real jam: the machine ended in (6,6) **28 mm east of centre with the rear-right wheel against the east wall**, rotated **−5.07°** (camera, 5-frame spread 0.18°). The ROI search is **±40 px = ±23.5 mm**, so at 28 mm the bands were out of range, the FRONT ROI picked up a post, and the read came out `{N, E}` against a true `{E}` — `verify_cell` compared that against (6,6) and its two neighbours, matched none, and correctly said "lost". **The corridor clearance is 21.4 mm and the ROI search is 23.5 mm**, i.e. *the machine cannot lose its bands without also being at risk of touching a wall*. So the fix has to be a pair: measure the offset a way that does not depend on the ROIs, **and** refuse to drive on when that offset exceeds the clearance on a side that has a wall. Do one without the other and you get a machine that knows exactly which cell it is in and drives on with a wheel against a wall.
    - **Widening the ROI search does not help** — replayed on the jam frame at 40 / 60 / 80 / 100 / 120 px the four readings never moved (0.12 / 0.00 / 0.00 / 0.22). The wall it is touching is **occluded by the machine itself**; only the middle rows of the frame see it. More search range cannot find red that is not in the picture.
    - **The lattice phase does work, because it uses every red pixel instead of four boxes.** Walls and posts only ever sit on the 180 mm lattice, so the weighted circular mean of the red mass at the lattice frequency gives the offset mod 180 mm, folded into (−90, +90] because the machine is inside a cell. On the jam frame it returned **−27.9 mm** laterally against a hand-measured −28.0, and −3.4 mm fore-aft against −6.0. Two things are required and both were found by it failing first: **de-rotate by the measured yaw** (at 5° one wall moves 60 px across the frame and the 306 px period smears — the fore-aft reading went −17.3 → −3.4 mm), and **separate vertical from horizontal structure before profiling** (a wall spans exactly one pitch on the perpendicular axis, so it adds nothing but denominator and crushed the fore-aft confidence below the threshold — that axis read "unmeasurable" until the split). Posts belong to *both* profiles, since a post sits on both lines.
    - **The wall was not moved, and proving that needs a reference other than the wall itself.** Using the two posts on the west lattice line (−119 mm) and the outer wall to the east (+243 mm) — neither of them the one being touched — the machine is +28 mm east; the touched wall then sits at **+92 mm from the cell centre against a nominal 90**. So the maze did not need rebuilding. Reading the offset off the contacted wall would have been circular and would have proved nothing.
    - **The gyro and the camera disagreed in *sign***: the estimator's residual was **+5.30°** (CCW) and the machine is physically at **−5.07°** (CW). That is a third pattern — #81's sampling error makes the *point sample* the outlier while the continuous integral tracks the camera, and #103's rub puts both gyro numbers off *together in the same direction*. The along-track residual was normal (0.3 mm; #85's earlier post graze was 6.3 mm) and the cross-track was **9.3 mm against a normal 0.2–1.8**, so this was a lateral event. The contact direction is at least self-consistent: a reaction pointing west applied *behind* the centre of mass gives `τ_z = r_x·F_y < 0`, i.e. the clockwise rotation observed. **What cannot be decided from this data is the order of events** — whether it rotated CCW and was slammed back, or the gyro integrated something that never happened.
    - **This is the first guard trip since the #107 pairing, and it converts a bound into an estimate**: 1122 chained motions with 1 abort = **0.089 % per motion**, against 3 in 263 (1.1 %) for the configurations #107 rejected. The pairing is ~13× better and **not zero**, which is exactly what #107 said its zero-abort run could not establish.
  - **`axis_yaw` is mod 90°, so a big rotation is a trap, not a harder problem.** At 50° it confidently reads −40° and snaps to the wrong lattice, then drives straight at a wall. `RECOVERY_ABANDON_RAD` refuses above **30°**. The three recorded aborts (2.78 / 5.43 / 25.68°) are all inside it, but 25.68° has only 4.3° of margin. The BNO055's magnetometer could in principle resolve the fold, but three permanent-magnet steppers sit next to the IMU and their field moves with rotor position — and at 30° the free-standing walls are down and the run is over anyway, so it is not worth building.
  - **Recovery is capped at once per session** (`--max-recoveries`, default 1). A machine whose fault is not understood and keeps recurring is more dangerous to keep running than to stop. `--no-recover` restores the old behaviour. Recovery makes an unexplained failure cheap instead of waiting to explain it — and that was the right call even though the failure then turned out to be reachable: see the next bullet.
