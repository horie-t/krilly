# 運動制御・コーナー・速度調整 (Motion, corners & tuning)

停止しないコーナー (#80)、チェイン数・脚長の上限、ランプ (accel/decel) と動作点の決め方、旋回のオーバーシュート (#73)、異常停止の裾 (#107)。

> 新しい知見はこのページ (または該当ページ) に追記し、CLAUDE.md には足さない。

- **A holonomic corner is *looser* than the straight corridor it connects, so rounding it costs no clearance** (#80, `motion/corner.py`). Turning means changing the translation axis, so a corner is just one axis decelerating while the other accelerates: start the next leg when the remaining distance hits the **blend distance `d = v²/2a`** and the old axis's own decel envelope `sqrt(2ad)` equals `v_max` exactly — the command is continuous across the seam, with no special case. At the tuned point (v 0.24, a 0.8) `d = 36 mm`, the path bulges `d/4 = 9 mm` toward the inside, and the machine passes **34 mm** from the inner post — against **21.4 mm** for the same machine going straight down a corridor. The bulge goes into the quadrant between the edge you came in through and the edge you leave by, and both of those are open by construction: there is no wall there, only one post. The tightest point of the whole route is still the broadside pass down the corridor, which happens whether you round the corner or not, so `min_post_clearance_m ≥ corridor_clearance_m` holds for every `d` up to half a cell. Three consequences:
  - **The speed dips, it does not peak.** Through the corner the velocity vector falls to `v/√2`, so the wheels never see a new speed peak; what rises is *acceleration*, since both axes change at once. `check_limits` already sized the ramp for exactly that case (both axes at max accel = 1665 step/s² against the L6470's 2000), so the corner was already inside the driver's budget before anything was written.
  - **`d` grows with the square of the speed**, so it is the knob that breaks first when the machine gets faster: at v = 0.6 m/s it is 225 mm, i.e. the machine would start sliding sideways while still in the *previous* cell's corridor, where the wall it is sliding toward is very much present. `check_limits` refuses that (`> pitch/2`).
  - **A 180° reversal cannot be rounded** — reversing a velocity means passing through zero — so `CellMotion` stops for it and starts the next leg cleanly. Same-axis legs simply merge into a longer one.
- **The corner blend is real: the model predicts the hardware to 0.6 % at the primitive level, and one corner is no less accurate than stopping** (#80). Measured on the 5×5: `F,A` chained **1.85 s** against a predicted 1.86 s, and 1.12 + 1.12 s stopping; `F,A,F,D` chained **3.32 s** against a predicted 3.34 s. So a corner costs ~0.39 s less in motion alone, before counting the stop it removes. Accuracy on the single corner went the *right* way — camera-measured error 2.4/1.8 mm chained against 2.9/4.7 mm stopping, heading −0.59° against −1.65° — which is what you would expect if each stop/start transient costs something. **But chaining costs along-track distance, about 3.5–5 mm per corner, and the odometry cannot see it.** Paired on `F,A,F,D` (4 legs, 3 corners): chained **−10.6 mm** fore-aft against **−0.6 mm** stopping at every leg, with placement tilts of 0.87° and 0.22°, so the tilt is not the explanation (an earlier chained run placed 2.32° off gave −15.9 mm — same sign, same order). The leading suspect is **slip through the corner**: both axes ramp at 0.9 m/s² at once, so the body acceleration vector is 1.27 m/s², 1.4× a straight, and the estimator integrates *commanded* speeds so a shortfall is invisible to it. `cell_move_demo` now warns above 1° of placement tilt, which is what made this pair readable.
  - **The same pair is the sharpest measurement of the invisible rotation (#81) so far.** Chained, the camera and the gyro disagree by **1.154°** over the four legs; stopping at every leg, by **0.014°** — same path, same distance. That points at *continuous dynamic motion*, not distance travelled, as what generates it. The rate is sampled once per 20 ms control tick, which is worth suspecting when the corner's transients are this fast.
  - The practical consequence is a **cap, not a veto**: at one corner per chunk (`--chain-legs 2`) the 3–5 mm lands in the same band as the position corrections a run already applies at every stop (3–12 mm measured), so the next stop absorbs it. Three corners per chunk is 10–16 mm, over half the 21.4 mm corridor clearance, and that is not a margin to spend blind.
- **Chaining further than 2 is simulated, not measured.** Replaying the 5×5 route through `CellMotion` with a fake driver and 0.44 s of stop overhead gives 24.7 s at one leg per motion (hardware: 25.0) and **19.8 s chaining 2** (hardware: 20.0), then 18.1 s at 3, 17.3 s at 4 and **15.7 s chaining all 12**. The remaining 4 s is real but costs blind distance: position and heading fixes only happen while stopped, and the demo above puts the price at 3.5–5 mm of along-track error per corner. Raise `--chain-legs` one rung at a time and read the camera correction at each stop.
- **A leg had no length limit, and a 7-cell straight spends the whole corridor** (#85, `speed_run --max-leg-cells`, now **4**; `0` restores the old behaviour). `path_to_legs` run-length-encoded the route and `RunManager._route` passed it straight to the machine, so a corridor of any length ran as **one motion** — and the camera position fix and the path check only happen at the *head* of a motion. `--pass-cells` (default 2) caps only the **search**; it does nothing to the speed or return run. This stayed invisible because `mazes/excerpt8_2015.txt` has a longest leg of **4 cells**: 600+ chained motions on it, zero grazes. Two hand-modified boards with 6- and 7-cell corridors grazed walls in the first session.
  - **The measurement stops at 4 cells and 7 lands on the limit.** Corridor clearance is 21.4 mm per side; #87's aligned 4-cell strafes at 0.30 m/s (black floor) put the worst cross-track at **11.2 mm over 720 mm**. Scaled by distance: 5 cells 14.0, 6 cells 16.8, **7 cells 19.6 mm = 92 % of the clearance** — before the 2–3 mm the camera correction leaves and before any wall sitting a millimetre off. As an angle it is sharper: the heading that exhausts the clearance is **1.70° at 4 cells but 0.97° at 7**, and per-leg heading residuals measure 0.2–0.9° with a 1.42° outlier, so **at 7 cells the tolerable angle is inside the machine's own error distribution and at 4 cells it is outside it.** The path check is no help there either — it sees 1 cell ahead N/S and 2 E/W (#89), so a 7-cell leg drives its last 5 cells on the map alone.
  - **Splitting the leg does nothing on its own, and this is the trap.** `CellMotion.queue_move` merges same-direction legs into one longer one (only an orthogonal change is rounded), and the chunker grouped legs purely positionally, so `[N4, N3]` landing in one chunk drives exactly as `N7` did — no stop, no correction, and an estimate that now counts a motion the machine never makes. `chunk_legs` therefore **breaks whenever two consecutive legs share a direction**, and `RunManager.motions` uses the same function so the estimate and the machine cannot drift apart.
  - **The cap is not free, and its price is on 16×16, not on the 8×8.** The contest mazes' shortest paths have a longest leg of **median 11.5 cells, max 15, and 29 of 30 over 4**. Measured over the 30 with the current constants: cap 4 takes the speed runs **48 → 44**, the mazes that fit no speed run **2 → 3**, and the median speed run **58.8 → 62.0 s**; cap 5 and 6 cost 3 and 2 runs and shut out nobody extra. On `excerpt8_2015` it costs **exactly zero** (its longest leg is already 4). This is #87's trade-off taken the *other* way and deliberately: refusing a race costs one race, while a graze topples a free-standing wall (#65) and ends the run — and there is no measurement past 4 cells to argue from. **Raise it on evidence, not on the table**: `cell_move_demo --seq F5/F6/F7 --align --camera-pose` extends the cross-track measurement, and `--align` is not optional there (#87: a placement tilt alone is 12.6 mm per degree over 4 cells).
- **The speed knobs live in one place** (`motion/tuning.py`, `add_tuning_args` / `build_tuning`). Speed, accel and KVAL have to be identical across `cell_move_demo`, `search_run` and `speed_run` or the numbers you tune in one don't apply to the others. The three layers are not independent, and `check_limits()` checks the couplings against the real kinematics — **run it before raising anything**: `CellMotionConfig`'s decel is clamped to the `RampLimits` ramp; the ramp must stay under the L6470 `ACC/DEC` register or the driver becomes the limit and stops overshoot; and the peak commanded wheel speed must stay under `MAX_SPEED` or it clips and the three-wheel speed ratio breaks (= the path curves). `L6470Profile.acc_steps_s2` is 2000 because the tuned ramp (0.9 m/s² + 8 rad/s²) peaked at ~1640 step/s²; the ramp is now 0.7 (#107) so there is more room than that.
- **Nothing slows down for a corner, and the two couplings that decides are `accel` vs `decel`** (#107, `RampLimits` now **0.7** and `CellMotionConfig.decel_mps2` **0.6**). `_command` uses one `v_max` for both the new primary axis and the old axis it is closing out, so a rounded corner (#80) is **entered and left at 100 % of `v_max`**; the velocity vector dips to ~76 % mid-corner purely because the two axes swap linearly, not as a deliberate slow-down. What rises is acceleration: both axes change at once, so the body sees `sqrt(accel² + decel²)` — and **that is independent of `v_max`**, which is why "the corner demands more at 0.30" was the wrong way to read it. Two things fall out, both measured on the 8×8:
  - **The corner acceleration was what aborted 1 run in 5.** At 0.9/0.8 it is 1.20 m/s² and the machine rotated at the corner exit until the 2.5° `--max-heading-residual` guard fired; the worst chained motion of a *completed* session was 2.37°, i.e. 95 % of the guard. At 0.7/0.6 (0.92 m/s²) the worst of 56 chained motions is **1.08°** with nothing over 1.5°, and the chained along-track residual's worst fell 9.0 → 2.0 mm. 0.6/0.6 (0.85 m/s²) measured the same on heading over 280 motions, so **0.92 is already enough** — but see the next point for why 0.6/0.6 is not the setting. The cost is the blend growing 56 → 75 mm (inner-post clearance 25.9 → 21.9 mm, still over the corridor's 21.4 mm) and the speed run 19.3 → 20.0 s (+4 %). **The middle of the distribution barely moves; what the change removes is the tail** — that is the signature to look for, since the failure was 1-in-5, not a general degradation.
  - **`decel` must be strictly below `accel`; equality costs 3 mm on every move.** The decel envelope `sqrt(2·decel·r)` demands *exactly* `decel` of deceleration, so an equal ramp leaves zero margin to follow it and the machine coasts past the tolerance band. Measured: 0.6/0.6 (0 % margin) overshoots a 1-cell move by **+3.00 mm** (140 hardware motions, range ±3.10, sitting just under `retry_pos_tol_m` = 4.0 mm so no retry fires and it simply stays there), against **+0.20 mm** at 0.7/0.6 (14 % margin) and +0.36 mm at the old 0.9/0.8 (11 %). The simulation reproduces it to 0.03 mm, so this is checkable off-Pi. **`check_limits` cannot catch it** — it only refuses `decel > accel`, and equality is legal to it. Keep ≥10 % margin. This one was in the hardware data of the first five sessions and was missed because only the *chained* residuals were being read; the single-leg motions are where it shows.
- **How the operating point was found** (#21, and how to redo it): raise one axis at a time, running `cell_move_demo --seq F4 --camera-pose --camera-yaw` at each rung and reading the time, the camera drift and the L6470 fault flags. Straight: 0.12/0.18/0.24/0.30 m/s gave 6.25/4.27/3.29/2.69 s over 4 cells (theory to 0.5 %), distance error ±2 mm up to 0.18 and −5 ± 4 mm at ≥0.24; 0.30 buys only 0.5 s per 4 cells over 0.24 and eats the `MAX_SPEED` margin, so **0.24 wins**. Turns: 1.5/2.0/2.5 rad/s gave 1.83/1.56/1.67 s, heading error (4 turns) −0.60/−0.36/−0.50°, so **2.0 wins** — 2.5 is not faster and is less accurate. No `STEP_LOSS`/`OCD` at any rung, so KVAL was never the limit; the limits here are slip and mechanical play, not torque. **"0.24 wins" was decided on the wrong scale, and 0.30 m/s is now the default** (#87 argued it, #103 measured both speeds and adopted it): 0.5 s per 4 cells is nothing per move, but over a whole 7-minute session it is the difference between **3 of the 30 contest mazes never getting to race and none of them** (total speed runs 38 → 54, median races per maze 1 → 2, simulated on the *measured* constants of both speeds at `chain-legs 2` — not on an assumed scaling). The two things #21 never tested were settled first. The peak commanded wheel speed at 0.30 is **453 step/s against the old `MAX_SPEED` 400, and it is the *lateral* case that clips** (forward stays under, which is why `--seq F4` looked fine), so `L6470Profile.max_speed_steps_s` went to **500** in the same change — `check_limits` catches exactly this, but note it **returns** its warnings rather than raising, so read the return value or a `try/except` will call 0.30/400 clean. And **since #76 half of every route is a strafe**, so the axis #21 never tested was the one that mattered — #87's aligned 4-cell strafes put 0.30 at least as accurate as 0.24 (mean |cross-track| 6.6 vs 8.2 mm). End to end on the 8×8 (#103, same day and pack, `--chain-legs 1`): search 33.2 → 31.2 s, speed run 27.6 → 25.6 s over four runs, zero L6470 faults, nothing aborted, identical map. **What it costs, both small and both known**: the corner blend grows with `v²` so 36 → 56 mm, taking the inner-post clearance 34 → 26 mm (still above the 21.4 mm the same machine has going straight down a corridor); and cross-track disturbance rejection weakens, because `v_cross_max` is a fixed 0.04 m/s and there is less time per cell — a simulated 10 mm kick leaves 2.6 mm instead of 2.0 mm. Raising `v_cross_max` to match has not been measured.
- **Corners now slow down: `corner_v_max` 0.20 m/s, `corner_accel_mps2` 0.4 (simulated, not yet measured)** (2026-10-17, `--corner-speed` / `--corner-accel`, `0` restores the bullet above). At the venue (16×16, black floor, EV −2, 0.30 m/s) the wheels stopped turning smoothly during `S3->W1` (−6.4 mm along-track, +1.74°) and the next chained motion `S1->E1` rotated **27.7°**. Before this, nothing slowed for a corner — it was entered and left at `v_max` with the body seeing ~0.93 m/s² for the whole blend. Now the old axis decelerates at the normal `decel` to `corner_v_max` by the blend point, both axes then swap at `corner_accel` (old `v_c → 0`, new `0 → v_c`), and the normal ramp takes the new axis back to `v_max`. **Geometry**: the blend path is the same parabola as `corner_path`, `d = v_c²/2a_c`, radius of curvature **2d at the ends and d/√2 in the middle**: old 0.30/0.6 → d 75 mm, R 150 → 53 mm (sim 57 mm), mid-corner speed 0.21 m/s; new 0.20/0.4 → d 50 mm, R 100 → 35 mm (sim 34 mm), mid speed 0.14 m/s. Simulated on S3→W1: median body acceleration in the corner **0.93 → 0.58 m/s²**, ticks over 0.8 m/s² 20 → 3 (the 2–3 left are the blend exit, where the old axis's tail and the new axis's ramp overlap — present before too), **+0.24 s per corner** (≈ +3 s per 16×16 speed run at `--chain-legs 2`; `RunManager`'s constants have not been re-fitted). Note the radius *shrinks* when slowing at a fixed acceleration (`R ∝ v²/a`) — what drops is the speed and the force, not the tightness. **Measure before trusting**: `cell_move_demo --seq S3,W1 --chain` style runs at 0 vs the default, reading the residual tail as in #107.
  - **The same venue run exposed a crash in recovery**: after the 27.7° rotation the lattice fix asked to shift the ROIs by (−147, +57) px, the left neighbour's ROIs went off-frame, and a negative `Roi.x` sliced the *opposite* edge of the mask (Python wraparound) into an empty mean → `IndexError`. `_band_profile` now clamps to the frame, an off-frame band reads as saturated, and `recover_pose` aborts ("持ち上げて置き直す") if any of the four own-cell ROIs would leave the frame.
- **Two factors were each necessary and neither was sufficient, which is why changing one at a time said "nothing matters"** (#107). The 1-in-5 abort (`--max-heading-residual` firing on a rounded corner) survived a rubber trim, a full roller reshuffle, the ramp change and a W1 wheel swap when those were judged **one at a time by counting aborted sessions**. Counting aborts is a weak instrument: at a ~20 % session rate you need ~50 sessions per arm to see a 2× change, and ~20 sessions of it ordered nothing. **The per-motion residual tail is the instrument that works** — every chained motion is a sample, so one session is ~56 of them instead of one:

  | wheel + ramp | chained motions | median | **max** | >1.0° | aborts |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | old wheel, 0.9/0.8 | 12 | 0.88° | **25.68°** | 6 | 1 |
  | old wheel, 0.7/0.6 | 178 | 0.36° | **2.78°** | 2 | 1 |
  | **new wheel, 0.7/0.6** | 280 | 0.44° | **1.12°** | 1 | **0** |
  | new wheel, 0.9/0.8 | 73 | 0.50° | **5.43°** | 4 | 1 |
  | **new wheel, 0.7/0.6 — 25-run session** | **336** | 0.47° | **0.94°** | **0** | **0** |

  **Only the pair clears the tail**: 616 motions with one reading over 1.0° and no abort, against 263 motions with twelve and three for the other three cells. Softening the ramp alone still produced 2.78°; the new wheel alone still produced 5.43°. **A 2×2 read one factor at a time cannot see an interaction** — each row looked like "no effect" because the other factor was still broken. The medians say nothing (0.36–0.50° everywhere); it is entirely a tail effect, which is also why the middle of the distribution was no guide. What this does *not* establish is that the rate is zero, and it is not: **1986 chained motions have since produced exactly one abort** (#85), i.e. **0.050 % per motion** with a one-sided 95 % upper bound of 0.24 %, against 3 in 263 (1.14 %) for the configurations this replaced — a **23× improvement that is now an estimate rather than an absence**. Per session that is 4 % on the 8×8 and **6 % on a 16×16** (26 % if the true rate sits at the upper bound). The cause remains unidentified — it is not corner-specific either (a single straight leg produced +1.225°), and the one abort was a lateral event with the gyro and camera disagreeing in *sign* (see the contact bullet under #113).
- **The turn overshoot is a lag, but not the one #81 fixed — turns and translations fail for different reasons** (#73, `CellMotionConfig.gyro_delay_s`). Fixing the gyro sampling (#81) changed the turn overshoot not at all: measured after it, `Q,Q,Q,Q` at ω = 2.0 rad/s overshoots **4.5°** against 2.3–3.2° before. The single-run A/B says why — during a turn the point sample and the continuous integral agree to **0.2°** (against 1.32° in a translation), because a turn's rate is large and slowly varying, so there is nothing fast to alias. **The two issues were never the same root cause.**
  - **The overshoot is `≈ 1.5° + 26 ms × ω`, not proportional.** Measured: 4.5° at ω = 2.0 / dt = 20 ms, 3.75° at ω = 2.0 / dt = 10 ms, 3.03° at ω = 1.0 / dt = 20 ms. Halving ω does not halve the overshoot, so there is a fixed part the lead compensation cannot touch.
  - **The mechanism is that the lag shifts the whole decel schedule, not just the final test.** Simulating a lagged gyro: when the *estimated* remaining crosses zero the machine is still turning at 39 °/s, because the envelope has been computing `sqrt(2·a·r)` on a too-large `r` the whole way down and the ramp cannot make up the difference. That is why the overshoot scales with `ω_max` rather than with the rate at the moment of termination.
  - **`--dt 0.01` speeds turns up by 0.29 s (1.83 → 1.55 s), and it is entirely a retry effect.** The finer tick makes the overshoot smaller (4.5 → 3.75°), which shortens the retry. Translations are *completely* insensitive to `dt` (1 cell 1.12 → 1.13 s, 4 cells 3.36 → 3.38 s, simulated) because they never retry. Do not reach for a faster control loop expecting a general speed-up.
  - **`gyro_delay_s = 0.026` is the fitted delay and it lands where the model says.** It subtracts `delay × measured rate` from the turn's remaining (a first-order lead inverting a first-order lag), in both the termination test and the decel envelope. Measured on hardware: **1.83 → 1.42 s per turn (−22 %), retries 4/4 → 2/4, overshoot 4.5° → 1.83/1.96°** — exactly the 1.5° fixed part the fit predicted would survive. The residual also stopped being *biased*: before, all four turns ended +1.5° short, so the error accumulated; after, they land ±0.9° with mixed signs. **Fit it from two ω values, never one** — with a fixed part in the sum, a single point gives the wrong delay, and too much lead stops the turn short.
  - **The surviving 1.5° is the ramp-down after the decision.** At the moment the (compensated) remaining crosses the tolerance the machine is still turning 20–39 °/s, and bringing that to zero at the 8 rad/s² ramp costs about a degree. No amount of lead removes it; it would take a gentler envelope tail.
  - **The payoff is small and that is the point.** Since #76 the machine does not turn while running; turns survive only as the `gyro_scale_z` calibration procedure and as an escape route. This was worth finishing because that calibration is the only way to measure the gyro — not because a competition run gets faster.
