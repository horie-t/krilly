# 照明・露出・床と白/黄の壁 (Lighting, exposure, floors & white tops)

AE/ゲインの余裕、色温度、黒い床での BACK の白飛びと `--ev -2`、始点/終点の白・黄の壁上面 (#125/#126)。

> CLAUDE.md から移した知見ページ。索引は [README.md](README.md)。

- **Auto-exposure eats the brightness axis, so illuminance is not what breaks the red — colour temperature is** (#78). Measured on the room's ceiling light (NLEH08002E-LC: LED, 2700–6500 K, 4299 lm, dimmable 5–100 %, Ra85), machine parked in one cell and the camera **restarted for each condition** — the AE/AWB lock is taken 0.5 s after startup, so a lighting change needs a restart, not another shot.
  - **6500 K at 100 / 30 / 5 % dimming: frame median V 157 / 156 / 156, front band 0.401 / 0.402 / 0.401.** A 20× change in lamp output moved nothing, because AE simply re-locks to the same image brightness.
  - **What AE spends is gain, and it is the only thing it can spend.** Logged at the lock: exposure **32.7 ms in all three conditions** while gain went **2.16 → 6.21 → 10.67**. Exposure cannot move because `create_video_configuration` defaults `FrameDurationLimits` to 30 fps (33333 µs); the sensor would allow up to 112 s. So the whole defence against darkness is `AnalogueGain` **1.12–16.0, about 14×**, and the room's 5 % setting already sits at **0.6 stops from the ceiling** (100 % is 2.9 stops, 30 % is 1.4). Inferring this from inter-frame noise instead (it rose only 0.94 → 1.33 and said "exposure, not gain") got it backwards — **read the control, don't estimate it from the picture.** `Camera.headroom_stops()` reports it and the driving scripts print it at startup; below 1 stop it warns. Note the readings were still healthy at gain 10.67 (0.401 / 0.453), so **gain noise is not what breaks the mask** — running out of gain is. **The opposite lever exists too, and the black competition floor is what will need it** (#100): a dark floor makes AE open up and blow out the bright wall tops, which is exactly how #56 lost a wall (S dropped to 46–54). `--ae-constraint Highlight` tells libcamera to expose for the highlights instead, and `--exposure` / `--gain` force the lock outright. On the wood floor, where nothing is clipping, forcing **8 ms against AE's 32.7 ms — 4× less light — drops the bands from 0.41/0.55/0.45 to 0.17/0.53/0.20**, which is the expected and correct behaviour: exposing down only helps when the wall tops are actually blown out. **A forced exposure takes a few frames to reach the sensor**, so the first capture after the lock still carries the old one — measuring this with `--measure 1` produced "6.5× less light changed nothing", and `--measure 5` showed frame #01 at 0.41 and #02–#05 at 0.17. `lock_exposure` now settles and discards a frame; check any exposure claim over ≥3 frames. All four camera options come from one shared `add_camera_args` / `camera_kwargs` (`hal/camera.py`), the same pattern as `motion/tuning.py` — four scripts each defining their own copy is how they drift apart. If a venue needs more light, the lever is `FrameDurationLimits` (`--max-frame-duration`, default kept at 33.3 ms): wall readings are taken at a standstill, so a longer frame duration costs latency per stop and nothing else. **It buys 0.6 stops and that is all** — measured under one fixed light, changing only the cap: 33.3 ms → exposure 32.7 ms / gain 3.51; **100 ms → 50.0 ms / gain 2.29**; 200 ms and 400 ms → also 50.0 ms / 2.29, because libcamera's AE stops lengthening exposure at 50 ms and puts the rest into gain (`AeExposureMode=Long` only reaches 57.4 ms, another 0.2 stops). So set it to 100 if a venue is dark, never higher, and if 0.6 stops is not enough the answer is more light, not more configuration.
  - **What did move is colour, and dimming *improves* it.** The front band's saturation went 127 → 135 → 153 and its hue 157 → 158 → 162 (nearer pure red) as the room got darker: bright light washes out the glossy wall tops, the same failure as #56. So **cool + bright is the worst case**, which is exactly what a gymnasium is. At 2700 K everything sits at H 175–177 and reads strongest (0.42 / 0.48) — warm light is easy, the whole risk is at the cool end.
  - **The bright-cool case is where `h2_lo = 140` would have driven the machine into a wall.** Replaying the same frames through the old mask, the palest wall reads **0.046 at 6500 K / 100 %** — *under* the 0.08 threshold, i.e. a missed wall — then 0.083 at 30 % and 0.286 at 5 %; with 125 it reads 0.348–0.453 throughout. The "pale wall" problem and the "cool lighting" problem are one problem seen from two sides, and neither is a threshold problem.
  - **This lamp does not flicker** (5 frames: red fraction spread 0.00, axis-yaw spread 0.086°) and **it cannot simulate a venue.** Mercury vapour is Ra≈40 with a spiky spectrum and poor red rendering against this lamp's Ra85, and it flickers at 100/120 Hz; no colour-temperature setting reproduces that. The point of the ladder is the *slope*, so that one run at the venue can be read immediately — not to imitate the venue.
- **The black competition floor washes out the BACK band alone, and it is saturation, not clipping** (#100). Measured on a 1×5 corridor built twice in the same place under the same light, wood floor then official black floor. Three edges do not care and one nearly fails:

  | edge | wood W | black W | wood E | **black E** |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | front | 0.40 | 0.42 | 0.41 | 0.43 |
  | **back** | **0.53** | **0.40** | **0.46** | **0.13** |
  | left/right | 0.46 | 0.45 | 0.44 | 0.45 |

  0.13 is **1.6× the 0.08 threshold** — below the 0.24 that broke a wall in #88. The mechanism, read off the saved frames by measuring the band's own pixels rather than the frame average: on the black floor AE opens from gain 3.3 to **7.1**, and the BACK band — already the brightest of the four because the wall behind the machine catches the ceiling lamp at the mirror angle — goes to **V 210 at S 49**, so **half its pixels fall under `s_min = 50`**. Nothing clips (0.0 % of the band reaches V 250); it goes *pale*, exactly as #56 described on the old black panels. front and left sit at S 163–170 and never notice.
  - **`ExposureValue` is the lever, it is adaptive, and it takes −2 stops** (`--ev`, `hal/camera.py`). It tells AE to aim N stops darker, so unlike a hardcoded `--exposure` in milliseconds it still tracks the venue's lighting. Measured at both ends of the corridor, reading the band's own pixels:

    | | gain | back | band S | S < `s_min` | V < `v_min` |
    |---|---|---|---|---|---|
    | west EV 0 | 7.11 | 0.34 | 49 | 50 % | 0 % |
    | west EV −1 | 3.37 | 0.48 | 74 | 29 % | 0 % |
    | west EV −2 | 1.72 | 0.55 | 89 | 15 % | 0 % |
    | **east EV 0** | 8.33 | **0.09** | **42** | **80 %** | 0 % |
    | **east EV −1** | 3.94 | **0.17** | **42** | 69 % | 0 % |
    | **east EV −2** | 2.00 | **0.53** | 64 | 26 % | 20 % |

    **The east end is the case that decides it**: at EV 0 the back wall reads **0.09 against a 0.08 threshold** — 1.1×, i.e. one frame of noise from a missed wall and a collision — and EV −1 only reaches 0.17. **EV −2 is the setting.** Note the band's saturation does *not* improve from EV 0 to EV −1 (42 either way, only V falls 246 → 189) and then jumps to 64 at EV −2: the pale reading is the ISP's highlight compression, not sensor clipping (nothing reaches V 250), so it only lets go once the band is out of the compressed top of the tone curve.
  - **EV −2 sits between two cliffs, and that is why it is not a default.** Below it the BACK band loses saturation; past it the *dark* end starts costing pixels — at EV −2, 20 % of the back band is already under `v_min = 40` (0 % at EV −1). And on the **wood** floor exposing down 2 stops does not merely weaken the band — **it misses the wall**: with the 8×8 built on the flooring, the same BACK wall reads **0.59 at EV 0, 0.41 at EV −1 and 0.01 at EV −2**, against a 0.08 threshold, and the fore-aft position becomes unmeasurable entirely (both longitudinal edges below `OFFSET_MIN_FRACTION`). So the EV belongs to the floor, not to the machine: **default 0 on the wood floor, `--ev -2` on the black competition floor**, and read the four edges before every session rather than carrying the last setting over. The two floors want settings two stops apart, so this is not a knob to leave on.
  - **front and right never notice any of this** (0.42–0.46 at every EV on both floors), because their saturation *rises* as they darken (175 → 187 → 198) and stays far above the threshold. The BACK wall is the one behind the machine, catching the ceiling lamp at the mirror angle — the same specular mechanism as #65's "same wall read 0.00 facing east and 0.38 facing west". **Which edge suffers is a property of the lamp geometry, so at a venue it may not be BACK**; expose so that the worst edge survives rather than tuning for this one.
  - **`--ae-constraint Highlight` is too weak to matter**: it moved gain 8.26 → 5.95 (0.5 stops) and left back at 0.11. It was the obvious first guess and it was wrong.
  - **The two floors respond to the same knob in opposite directions**, which is what proves it is over-exposure and not something else: forcing 8 ms took back from 0.13 to 0.48 on the black floor and took front from 0.41 to 0.14 on the wood floor. Any exposure claim has to say which floor it was measured on.
  - **The posts hold the wall tops at the same height on both floors** (50 mm either way, socket or free-standing): the measured fore-aft offset was +5.3 mm on both, so the bands did not move and `CALIBRATED_BANDS` is valid on both. That was the other thing that could have gone wrong, and it did not.
- **Start and goal wall tops may be white or yellow, and only *shape* separates white from the floor** (#125, rule 2-1: 「始点の区画及び終点領域の区画の壁の上面は赤色、白色または黄色とする」; `--white-tops`, `red_wall.white_yellow_mask`). Red-only perception reads the goal's seven boundary walls as open and drives into the first one; the path check cannot stop it, because it only stops on walls it *sees*. Measured on the black floor at EV −2 with the machine's top covered in matte felt:
  - **Neither brightness nor saturation separates white from the floor.** The black floor mirrors the ceiling lamp at V 73–108, and the sheen's saturation moved **S 46–57 → 11–30 between two sessions** with the gain unchanged, i.e. straight into white's S 6–39. The first threshold (V ≥ 100, S ≤ 40) scored 0.016 on one no-wall shot and **0.245 on the next** — above even the path-check floor. The fix is a 1-D **top-hat across the band** (41 px): a wall top is a ~20 px stripe, the sheen a broad patch, so "brighter than its surroundings" keeps one and deletes the other. Weakest white wall **0.46**, yellow **0.455**, no-wall max over 15 frames **0.006** (first version; see the next-but-one point for what the first real run changed).
  - **`white_s_max` is not there for the floor, it is there for the felt cover**: the felt (camera sees H 98 / S 255) sits inside the BACK search range, and without the saturation cap its edge reads 0.19–0.39. The cover is *meant* to be in the ROIs — it replaces the machine with something no mask accepts; moving it out would expose the wiring. The bare metal camera-mast rods crossed FRONT/RIGHT and were taped green for the same reason.
  - **The first real run drove into the white goal wall, and it was the saturation cap, set on the white side of the gap** (`mazes/white_goal3.txt`, `--goal 2,2`). The cap was 60 because white had measured S 6–39. On the 3×3, with more red and yellow in frame, the AWB lock gave white a blue cast: **S 64–73, V 200**, so the wall at (1,2) read **0.05**, the planner went east, and the machine hit it at 17.85° of uncommanded rotation (the `--max-heading-residual` guard stopped it; no damage at that speed). The black floor itself read S 105–120 in that session against ~50 before. This is the same lesson the floor sheen had already taught, applied one step too late: **a neutral surface's saturation belongs to the white-balance lock, not to the surface.** The cap went to 80 — and **that repeated the mistake one step up**: the very next run had a different white wall (the start cell's west) at **S median 77, top 5 % at 86**, so 27 % of its band fell over the cap and it read **0.29** against 0.45 for the others. White saturation varies *per wall* as well as per lock, so the cap must not be fitted to the whitest white seen so far; it is now **120, midway between white's measured top (86) and the tape (180)**, and a test pins that it stays ≥ 30 from both. Re-judged over every frame: walls ≥ 0.419, open edges ≤ 0.005, no red reading changed. Two more fixes came out of the same four frames: (1) the **floor-panel seam**, a 3 px bright line (H 35 / S 30 / V 100), read 0.052 on an open edge, between the clear limit and the threshold; an opening 9 px across the band (`min_width_px`) removes lines and keeps the ~20 px wall top. (2) At the start cell the BACK search range overhangs the south outer wall onto the **wood floor outside the maze, which reads yellow 0.999** and displaced a perfectly good red 0.55, taking the position fix with it; white/yellow is now only consulted when red is **below its threshold**, so a confident red wall is never replaced. After all three (at cap 80): white/yellow walls ≥ 0.446, open edges ≤ 0.002, every red reading bit-for-bit unchanged, and the goal's white wall is seen a cell early as a far neighbour band (0.75, where it had read 0.04).
  - **Wall presence only, never position** (`BandReading.source`). White/yellow readings feed `detect`, the path check and the neighbour tri-state, but `cell_offset` and `lateral_shift_px` ignore them: the top-hat can pick up a wall's white *side face*, and a position fix physically moves the machine. In the start/goal cells that axis simply goes uncorrected. Red and white/yellow are measured separately and the stronger wins, rather than OR-ed, so a red wall's white side face can never fatten a red band — red readings are bit-for-bit what they were.
  - **Black floor only, and off by default.** At EV −2 the wood floor lands entirely inside the yellow mask. Pass `--white-tops` together with `--ev -2`; both belong to the floor, not the machine.
  - **Off-centre holds, and a white-only cell makes more stops rather than fewer.** With the machine ~13 mm into the glossy side, two white walls read 0.45 / 0.51 and the two open edges 0.00 (`survey/felt/wy_a_s`). Because the machine's lateral offset is measured from *red* bands only, a cell whose side walls are all white cannot place its far neighbour band, so those far edges stay **undecided** (#89's guard) — the cell is a normal stop, which is the safe direction. The neighbour ROIs do pick up whatever is off the board: a floor-panel seam reads yellow 0.02–0.03 on `right:back` (under the 0.04 clear limit, not by much) and the wood floor beyond the test board fills `left:left`.
  - **The re-run reached the goal cleanly** (`survey/white_goal2`, same board, same command): the white wall was recorded a cell early as a far neighbour band (**0.81** from (0,2)), (1,2) read it again at 0.47 and turned south, and the map matched truth exactly — 55 labels, **zero misses, zero false alarms, weakest wall 5.2× the threshold**. In the goal cell the position fix reports "no wall to measure", as designed. `search_run` now marks white/yellow readings with `w` in its wall log.
  - **The start cell is seeded from the rules, and only with walls** (#126, `Explorer.seed_start`, on by default with `--white-tops`; `--seed-start` / `--no-seed-start` force it). Rule 2-3 gives the start one opening, toward which the machine is placed, so the other three edges are written as walls and marked `observed` (so `verify_cell` can use them). **The opening itself is never written as "no wall"**: the home boards are not all competition-shaped (`excerpt8*` leave the start's east open, `twisty8` closes its north), and seeding walls fails safe there — a detour, or `Unreachable` at once on `twisty8` — where seeding an opening would drive into a wall nobody has looked at. With white tops the camera read the start's white walls anyway (west 0.46 / east 0.49 / yellow south 0.98 once the saturation cap was right), and `axis_yaw` **was** measurable at the start (+1.2°, +2.2° — from the red walls of the neighbouring cells), so the issue's "don't use the camera at the start" was dropped. What the start cannot give is a **position** fix.
  - **An unfixable start silently doubles the chain length.** Position is corrected only at the head of a chunk (`execute_legs`), so with no fix at the start the blind stretch runs from the head of the return's last chunk to the end of the speed run's first chunk: up to **2 + 2 = 4 legs, i.e. `--chain-legs 4`**, which #80 said not to spend blind. Measured on `white_goal3` (N1→E2 out of the start, read at (2,1)): red start −2.3 / −5.9 / −8.2 / −5.9 mm, white start −9.4 / −14.1 / −15.2 / −7.0 / −8.2 / −4.7 mm. (Pointed out by the owner, not by me — I had proposed "stop at (0,1) to get a fix" without noticing the fix was already missing.) **The goal has the same problem, mirrored and worse**: its 2×2 has no interior walls or posts (rule 9), so with a white boundary no goal cell has a red wall of its own, and a 16×16 session crosses both ends every cycle. Fix: `RunManager.unfixable` remembers cells where a stop gave no position fix (`speed_run`'s `correct_at` calls `mark_fix`; the start is learned at the first search stop, the goal on arrival, both before any return/speed route is chunked), and `RunManager.chunks` — used by **both** `speed_run` and `motions`/`estimate_s(origin=…)` — cuts the chunk leaving an unfixable origin and the chunk entering an unfixable destination to one leg. The blind stretch is back to `chain_legs`, at the price of up to one extra stop per endpoint per run, and the estimate pays for it. Mid-route wall-less cells at a chunk head have the same property but are route-dependent and pre-existing (#85's blind runs); they are out of scope here. **Measured on `white_goal3`, 5 runs each end**: with a white start the speed run became [N1] [E2→N1] and the stop at (0,1) — after the blind S1 + N1 — read **±4.7 mm at worst**; with a white goal the return became [S1] [W2→S1] and its first stop at (2,1) read **≤ 3.5 mm**. Both cost **nothing** on this board (same number of motions: 4.7 s and 4.6 s per speed run as before), because the extra cut only moved where the corner falls. The same data exposed an unrelated **#80 effect**: a chunk that *ends in a corner after an east leg* (E2→N1) left **−9.4 to −13.5 mm of east overshoot** at the next stop, against −5.9 mm for N1→E2, which ends on the east leg's own terminal decel — i.e. the along-track error of a leg that blends into a corner instead of stopping, and here it overshoots rather than falling short as #80 recorded. The tail rule already keeps that out of an unfixable goal (the chunk into it is a single leg that stops).
  - **Never pre-check white with the camera started somewhere else.** The pre-run `wall_detect --measure` at (1,2) read that wall 0.51 and the run then read it 0.05: the AWB lock is taken at camera start-up, and a start from (0,0) (more red and yellow in frame) tinted white blue. A check is only representative if the camera starts where the run starts.
  - **Not yet measured**: the venue's own white (matte or glossy, and where its floor mirrors the lamp). That is the trial run's job (issue step 5: one search run with `--save-frames`).
